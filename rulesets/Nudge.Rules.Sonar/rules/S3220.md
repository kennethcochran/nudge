# S3220 — Method calls should not resolve ambiguously to overloads with "params"

## Why
When overloads differ only by `params`, a call like `Foo(null)` — or even
`Foo()` — can bind to an overload the author never intended, and adding a new
overload later can silently rebind existing calls. The resolution is fragile
and unreadable: the reader must simulate the compiler's tie-breaking to know
what runs.

## Do this
1. Disambiguate the call explicitly: cast the argument to the intended
   parameter type, or restructure so the intent is obvious.
2. Longer term, avoid overload sets whose members differ only in
   params-ness; give the variants distinct names or signatures.

## AVOID
- Do not "fix" the ambiguity by adding another overload. More candidates
  make overload resolution less predictable, not more — you are feeding the
  problem.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
