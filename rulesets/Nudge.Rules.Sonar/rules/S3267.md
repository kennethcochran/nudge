# S3267 — Loops should be simplified with "LINQ" expressions

## Why
The rule fires on `foreach` loops whose entire body is a filter or
projection into another collection — the imperative loop restates what
`Where` / `Select` / `ToList` say in one line. The LINQ form is shorter,
harder to get subtly wrong (no forgotten `Add`, no off-by-one), and signals
intent ("this is a transformation") instead of mechanics.

## Do this
1. Identify what the loop body does: filtering becomes `.Where(predicate)`,
   transforming becomes `.Select(projection)` — chain them when the loop
   does both.
2. Rewrite as a single expression, e.g.
   `var adults = people.Where(p => p.Age >= 18).ToList();`, preserving the
   result type the callers expect.
3. Keep the loop only if the body has side effects beyond building the
   collection (logging, mutating external state) — then the rule shouldn't
   have fired, so double-check the diagnostic location.

## AVOID
- Do not convert the `foreach` to an indexed `for` loop — that keeps the
  imperative restatement and adds indexing noise on top.
- Do not keep the loop and add a comment explaining it "could be LINQ";
  the fix is the rewrite, not the apology.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
