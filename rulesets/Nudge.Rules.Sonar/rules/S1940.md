# S1940 — Boolean checks should not be inverted

## Why
Inverted checks like `if (x == false)` or `if (!(a == b))` force a double
mental negation: the reader parses the comparison, then inverts it. `!=`
and `!` exist precisely so the code can say what it means directly — `x != y`
reads as "x differs from y" with no decoding step — and inverted forms are
where `==` / `!=` typos hide.

## Do this
1. Replace `== false` with `!`, `!= true` with `!`, `!(a == b)` with
   `a != b`, `!(a != b)` with `a == b`.
2. For negated compound conditions, apply De Morgan's law explicitly
   (`!(a && b)` → `!a || !b`) and re-read the result to confirm it still
   means what the original meant.
3. After uninverting, check the branch bodies still align with the condition
   — an inverted check sometimes had its then/else arms written to match
   the inversion.

## AVOID
- Do not "fix" `x == false` by writing `!(x == true)` — that's an inversion
  wrapped around a gratuitous comparison, the worst of both rules.
- Do not leave the inverted check and swap the then/else branches to
  compensate; branch-swapping preserves the confusing condition instead of
  fixing it.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
