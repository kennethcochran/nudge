# S2197 — Modulus results should not be checked for direct equality

## Why
In C#, the remainder takes the sign of the dividend: `-7 % 2` is `-1`, not
`1`. So `x % 2 == 1` is false for negative odd numbers, and the "is odd"
check silently misclassifies half its inputs. Direct equality against a
positive remainder is only correct for non-negative values.

## Do this
1. Test oddness with `x % 2 != 0` (evenness with `x % 2 == 0`) — correct for
   every value including negatives.
2. For general remainder checks, compare against the sign-aware range instead
   of a single positive constant.
3. Add negative-number cases to the tests covering the check.

## AVOID
- Do not wrap with `Math.Abs(x) % 2 == 1` — it overflows on `int.MinValue`
  (`Abs` of `MinValue` is still negative) and obscures the intent; the
  `!= 0` form is correct for all values.
- Do not "fix" it by clamping inputs to non-negative upstream — the check
  itself was wrong, and the next caller won't know about your undocumented
  precondition.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
