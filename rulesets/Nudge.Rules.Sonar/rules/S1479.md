# S1479 — "switch" statements with many "case" clauses should have only one statement

## Why
A `switch` with many multi-statement `case` clauses is a
method wearing a trench coat: each case is really its own little
function, but they're all crammed into one body where they share
locals, can't be named, can't be tested independently, and can't be
reused. Long case bodies are where switch statements go to rot.

## Do this
1. Extract each case body into its own well-named private method that
   says what the case does.
2. Leave each `case` clause as a single statement: the call to its
   method.
3. If several cases share logic, the extracted methods make that
   sharing visible — merge or delegate instead of duplicating.

## AVOID
- Do not collapse the body into one mega-statement (nested ternaries,
  a single call to a 200-line helper that keeps all the logic inline) —
  one statement per case means the logic moved into a named method, not
  that the semicolons were deleted.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
