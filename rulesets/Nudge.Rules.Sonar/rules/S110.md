# S110 — Inheritance tree of classes should not be too deep

## Why
Deep hierarchies are brittle: a change at the top ripples unpredictably
downward, and understanding any leaf requires holding the whole chain in your
head. Deep trees usually mean inheritance is being used for code reuse rather
than polymorphism — and reuse-through-inheritance is the most rigid kind.

## Do this
1. Favor composition: extract the shared behavior into collaborator classes
   injected through the constructor.
2. Collapse levels that add no behavioral distinction of their own.
3. Where polymorphism is genuinely needed, prefer interfaces over deep base
   classes.
4. Re-run the tests; composition changes should be behavior-preserving.

## AVOID
- Do not "fix" it by splitting one deep hierarchy into two hierarchies joined
   by a seam — the depth is still there, just harder to see. Flatten with
   composition instead.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
