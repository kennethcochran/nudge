# S3449 — Right operands of shift operators should be integers

## Why
In C#, the right operand of `<<`, `>>`, and `>>>` is
consumed as an `int`. Shifting by a `long` (or other non-`int` count)
silently truncates the count — and for `long` counts above 31/63 the
truncation changes the result with no warning. The shift count's type
should say what the operation actually uses.

## Do this
1. Find where the non-`int` shift count originates and validate it at
   the boundary: the valid range is 0–31 for `int` shifts, 0–63 for
   `long` shifts.
2. Clamp or reject out-of-range counts explicitly, then shift with an
   `int` value.
3. If the count comes from untrusted or computed data, the range check
   is load-bearing — don't skip it.

## AVOID
- Do not cast inline (`x << (int)count`) without a range check when
  the count can exceed the valid range — the truncation then happens
  silently instead of loudly.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
