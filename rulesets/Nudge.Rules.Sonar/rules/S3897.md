# S3897 — Classes that provide "Equals(<T>)" should implement "IEquatable<T>"

## Why
A typed `Equals(T)` without `IEquatable<T>` is a trap:
generic collections and LINQ prefer the interface, so `List<T>.Contains`
and `Dictionary<TKey,TValue>` will box your value type (or miss your
typed overload for reference types) and use the slower, possibly
different `Equals(object)` instead. The typed method you wrote isn't
the one that runs.

## Do this
1. Declare `IEquatable<T>` on the class/struct.
2. Implement the typed `Equals(T)` directly — real comparison logic,
   no boxing — and have `Equals(object)` delegate to it.
3. Make `GetHashCode` consistent with the equality: equal objects must
   produce equal hash codes, or hash-based collections silently lose
   entries.

## AVOID
- Do not implement `IEquatable<T>.Equals` by casting to `object` and
  calling the boxed `Equals` — that defeats the entire purpose (avoiding
  boxing) and risks inconsistent semantics between the two overloads.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
