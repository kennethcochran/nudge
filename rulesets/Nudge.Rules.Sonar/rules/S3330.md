# S3330 — Cookies should have the "HttpOnly" flag

## Why
A cookie without `HttpOnly` is readable from `document.cookie`, so any injected
script — one stored-XSS payload — can exfiltrate session and auth cookies.
`HttpOnly` keeps the cookie out of JavaScript's reach, which blunts the most
common XSS payoff.

## Do this
1. Set `HttpOnly = true` on `CookieOptions` (or `cookie.HttpOnly = true` on
   `HttpCookie`) for every authentication/session cookie.
2. Apply it at the policy level (`CookiePolicyOptions`, `AddCookie`) so newly
   added cookies inherit it instead of relying on each author to remember.
3. Verify in the browser dev tools that the flag is actually emitted on the
   `Set-Cookie` header.

## AVOID
- Do not treat `Secure` or `SameSite` as substitutes — they govern transport
  and cross-site sending, not script access. A `Secure; SameSite=Strict`
  cookie without `HttpOnly` is still stolen by XSS.
- Do not scope the fix to one cookie while the actual auth ticket cookie stays
  readable — audit all `Set-Cookie` emissions.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
