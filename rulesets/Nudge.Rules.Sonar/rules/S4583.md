# S4583 — Calls to delegate's method "BeginInvoke" should be paired with calls to "EndInvoke"

## Why
Every `BeginInvoke` allocates an async result and holds resources until
`EndInvoke` runs — skipping it leaks, and the worker's exception surfaces
unpredictably (or never) instead of at a defined point. The APM pattern is a
contract: begin and end are two halves of one operation.

## Do this
1. Pair each `BeginInvoke` with its `EndInvoke` — typically in the
   `AsyncCallback`, or after blocking on the wait handle.
2. Handle exceptions from `EndInvoke`: that is where the worker thread's
   exception rethrows, and it's the defined place to observe it.
3. Better still, replace the APM pattern with task-based async
   (`Task.Factory.FromAsync` or a modern async API) and delete both halves.

## AVOID
- Do not call `EndInvoke` on a *different* delegate instance or from a
  fire-and-forget thread "later" — the pairing is per-operation; a stray
  `EndInvoke` doesn't release the right resources.
- Do not "pair" them by calling `EndInvoke` immediately in a blocking wait
  when the whole point was asynchrony — if you block, you didn't need
  `BeginInvoke` at all; use a synchronous call.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
