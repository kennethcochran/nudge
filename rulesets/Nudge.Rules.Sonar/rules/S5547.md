# S5547 — Cipher algorithms should be robust

## Why
The rule fires on DES, TripleDES, and RC2 (including their
`*CryptoServiceProvider` / `*Managed` spellings) — ciphers with 56/64-bit
effective keys or known cryptanalytic breaks that modern hardware
brute-forces or cracks outright. Encrypting with them means confidentiality
rests on an algorithm the industry retired years ago; data encrypted today
can be decrypted tomorrow.

## Do this
1. Replace the cipher with `Aes.Create()` (AES-128 or AES-256), keeping the
   same code shape — key/IV handling stays, only the algorithm changes.
2. For new code, use an authenticated mode (`AesGcm`) so tampering is
   detected, not just hidden.
3. Re-encrypt long-lived data that was protected with the old cipher; check
   persisted blobs and config values, not just the flagged call site.

## AVOID
- Do not "strengthen" DES by double-encrypting or XOR-ing extra key
  material around it — home-grown constructions around a broken primitive
  stay broken.
- Do not swap DES for TripleDES as the fix; the rule flags TripleDES too,
  and its 112-bit effective strength with 64-bit blocks is not a modern
  choice.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
