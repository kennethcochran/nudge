# S3869 — "SafeHandle.DangerousGetHandle" should not be called

## Why
SafeHandle.DangerousGetHandle() hands you the raw IntPtr without
incrementing the reference count: if the SafeHandle is finalized or disposed
on another thread while you use the raw handle, you operate on a recycled or
closed OS handle — use-after-free at the Win32 level. The 'Dangerous' prefix
is the documentation. This rule flags direct calls.

## Do this
1. Keep the handle inside the SafeHandle: use the SafeHandle overloads of your P/Invoke signatures, or bracket the raw use with DangerousAddRef() / DangerousRelease() in try/finally.
2. If a native API truly needs the raw value for the duration of a call, scope the AddRef/Release as tightly as possible around that call.
3. Re-audit every other raw-handle call in the interop layer; they travel together.

## AVOID
- Do not wrap the DangerousGetHandle call in try/finally and declare it safe — the race is with finalization on another thread, which your finally block does not prevent.
- Do not cache the IntPtr in a field 'so you only call it once'; that widens the vulnerable window instead of closing it.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
