# S112 — General or reserved exceptions should never be thrown

## Why
Throwing `Exception`, `SystemException`,
`ApplicationException`, or reserved runtime exceptions
(`NullReferenceException`, `OutOfMemoryException`, …) forces callers to
catch broadly or not at all: handlers can't distinguish your failure
from every other failure, and catching `Exception` to handle your case
swallows unrelated bugs. Reserved exceptions are the runtime's to
throw, not yours.

## Do this
1. Throw the most specific existing exception type that fits:
   `ArgumentNullException`, `ArgumentOutOfRangeException`,
   `InvalidOperationException`, `NotSupportedException`, …
2. If none fits, define a custom exception type that carries the facts
   a handler needs (which argument, what state) — deriving from
   `Exception` with real information, not an empty subclass.
3. Never throw the runtime's reserved exceptions yourself; let the
   runtime raise them.

## AVOID
- Do not create `MyCustomException : Exception` with no additional
  information and throw it in place of `Exception` — a renamed general
  exception is still general; the type must carry facts a handler can
  act on.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
