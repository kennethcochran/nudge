# S4144 — Methods Should Not Have Identical Implementations

## Why
Two methods with byte-for-byte identical bodies are a maintenance trap: a bug
fixed in one is forgotten in the other, and a reader can't tell whether the
duplication is intentional (same behavior by coincidence) or a copy-paste that
drifted. If the behavior is truly meant to be the same, there should be one
method. If it's meant to differ, the difference should be visible.

## Do this
1. If the two methods are meant to do the same thing, extract the shared body
   into one method with a name that says what it computes, and have both call
   it (or delete one if it's genuinely redundant).
2. If they're meant to differ, make the difference explicit — the divergence
   is the information the duplication was hiding.
3. After the change, each remaining method should have exactly one reason to
   change.

## AVOID
- Do not make a trivial, meaningless edit to one copy (rename a variable,
  reorder two lines) just to make the implementations textually different.
  The duplication remains; you've only blinded the detector.
- Do not "fix" it by deleting one method when both are actually called with
  different intent — collapsing two concepts into one name is worse than
  duplication.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
