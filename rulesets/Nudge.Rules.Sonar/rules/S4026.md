# S4026 — Assemblies should be marked with "NeutralResourcesLanguageAttribute"

## Why
Without `[assembly: NeutralResourcesLanguage(...)]`, the resource manager
can't tell which culture the neutral (fallback) resources are written in,
so it probes satellite assemblies wastefully at startup and can misidentify
the fallback culture — a real startup-performance cost and a source of
wrong-language fallbacks in localized apps.

## Do this
1. Add `[assembly: NeutralResourcesLanguage("en")]` (or your development
   culture) to `AssemblyInfo.cs` or the project-level assembly attributes.
2. Use the culture the neutral `.resx` files are actually written in — not
   the culture you wish they were; a wrong declaration misroutes fallback
   lookups.
3. If the assembly has no resources at all, the attribute is still the
   documented expectation; add it rather than debating the edge case.

## AVOID
- Do not declare a culture that doesn't match the neutral resources (e.g.
  `"en"` when the resx is German) — the fallback chain then resolves to the
  wrong language.
- Do not "fix" it by adding satellite assemblies for the neutral culture
  instead; the attribute declares the fallback, satellites don't replace
  that declaration.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
