# S2156 — "sealed" classes should not have "protected" members

## Why
`protected` means "available to derived classes" — but a `sealed` class has
no derived classes, so a protected member is unreachable-by-design while
*advertising* extensibility. Readers waste time looking for overrides that
can't exist, and the member is effectively private while claiming otherwise.

## Do this
1. Change the member to `private` — that's what it actually is.
2. If the member was meant for future subclasses, the class shouldn't be
   sealed: remove `sealed` deliberately, or keep `sealed` and accept
   `private` — make it a conscious choice either way.
3. Check for `protected internal` too — the same reasoning applies to the
   protected half.

## AVOID
- Do not remove `sealed` just to legitimize the `protected` member —
  unsealing changes the type's inheritance contract for the whole codebase
  to fix a one-word modifier.
- Do not keep `protected` and add a comment saying "for future use"; future
  use requires unsealing, which is a separate deliberate decision.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
