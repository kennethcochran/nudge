# S8970 — Null-forgiving operators should not be used when nullable warnings are disabled

## Why
With `#nullable disable` the compiler performs no null analysis at all, so a
`!` operator asserts nothing and checks nothing — it is pure decoration. It
misleads the reader into believing a null-safety claim was verified when no
verification exists.

## Do this
1. Remove the `!` operators in the nullable-disabled context; they carry no
   meaning there.
2. If you want the safety the `!` pretends to give, enable nullable
   annotations for the file or project and let the compiler do the checking —
   then keep only the `!`s that survive real analysis, each with a reason.

## AVOID
- Do not "fix" this by enabling nullable annotations just to keep the `!`s
  and then sprinkling more to quiet the new warnings. Either do the
  annotation work properly or drop the operators.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
