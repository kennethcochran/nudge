# S1121 — Assignments should not be made from within sub-expressions

## Why
`if ((line = reader.ReadLine()) != null)` buries a state change inside a
condition — readers and future editors miss it, and `=`-vs-`==` typos become
invisible. The assignment deserves its own statement where it can be seen.

## Do this
1. Hoist the assignment above the statement: `var line = reader.ReadLine();`
   followed by `if (line != null)`.
2. In loops, restructure so the assignment happens at the top or bottom of the
   loop body rather than inside the `while` condition.

## AVOID
- Do not keep the assignment and wrap it in extra parentheses "for clarity".
  Parenthesizing the assignment does not move the side effect out of the
  sub-expression; the rule flags the assignment itself, not the missing
  parens.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
