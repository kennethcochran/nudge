# S4015 — Inherited member visibility should not be decreased

## Why
Hiding an inherited public member behind a less-visible one (private new
void Foo()) doesn't remove the base member — it creates two Foos: base-typed
callers still see the public one while derived-typed callers hit the private
wall. It's a polymorphism break that compiles. This rule flags visibility
decreases on inherited members.

## Do this
1. Keep the member at least as visible as the inherited one, or don't declare it at all.
2. If the goal is to block the operation on the derived type, that's a design smell — reconsider the inheritance (composition, or a narrower base contract) rather than hiding.
3. If you must hide, use 'new' with equal-or-greater visibility and document why the hiding exists.

## AVOID
- Do not restore visibility but change the member's semantics — a public new Foo() doing something unrelated to base.Foo() is the same trap with the volume turned up.
- Do not route around it with explicit interface implementation that silently drops the member for most callers.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
