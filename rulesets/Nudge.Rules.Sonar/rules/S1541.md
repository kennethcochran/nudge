# S1541 — Methods and properties should not be too complex

## Why
The rule measures cognitive complexity — nested conditionals, loops, catch
blocks, and boolean operator sequences that force the reader to hold
branching state in their head. Past the threshold, a method can't be fully
understood in one reading, which means reviewers approve logic they haven't
actually traced, and every future edit risks breaking an unconsidered path.

## Do this
1. Extract nested blocks into well-named private methods, one per conceptual
   step — the name documents what the block does, and the parent method
   becomes a readable sequence of steps.
2. Replace nested conditionals with guard clauses (early returns) to flatten
   the structure instead of indenting deeper.
3. When branches select behavior by a value, consider a `switch` expression
   or a lookup dictionary instead of an `if` / `else if` ladder; when they
   select by type, consider polymorphism.
4. Re-run the analyzer after each extraction — stop when the method reads as
   a flat sequence, not when you merely duck under the threshold.

## AVOID
- Do not merge conditions with `&&` / `||` just to drop the complexity
  count — one unreadable 200-character condition is worse than the nested
  version it replaced.
- Do not extract methods that take six parameters and return via `out` just
  to move the complexity elsewhere; if the extracted method needs that much
  context, the decomposition is wrong — the complexity moved, it didn't
  shrink.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
