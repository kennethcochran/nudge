# S1151 — "switch case" clauses should not have too many lines of code

## Why
A `case` clause with dozens of lines is a method that
was never extracted: it can't be named, can't be unit-tested in
isolation, and forces every reader of the switch to wade through one
case's internals to find the dispatch logic. Long cases are where
switch statements accumulate unrelated responsibilities.

## Do this
1. Extract the case body into a well-named private method.
2. Leave the `case` clause as the dispatch: a call (or two) to the
   extracted method.
3. If the extracted method is long too, that's a second signal — split
   it further rather than moving the wall of code verbatim.

## AVOID
- Do not shrink the line count with formatting tricks (multiple
  statements per line, collapsed braces, one giant ternary) — the rule
  counts complexity, and a reader still faces the same wall of logic.
  The body must move into a named method.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
