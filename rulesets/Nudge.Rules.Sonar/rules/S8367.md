# S8367 — Identifiers should not conflict with the C# 14 "field" contextual keyword

## Why
In C# 14, `field` inside a property accessor refers to the compiler-generated
backing field. A local, parameter, or member you named `field` in that scope
silently changes meaning — your identifier becomes the backing-field
reference. It compiles, it runs, and it does something completely different
from what the name says.

## Do this
1. Rename the identifier to something collision-free and descriptive
   (`fieldValue`, or better, a domain name like `rawName`).
2. Pay special attention inside property accessors, where the contextual
   meaning is active.

## AVOID
- Do not disambiguate with the `@field` escape. It compiles, but it keeps
  the trap in place for the next reader — rename the identifier instead.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
