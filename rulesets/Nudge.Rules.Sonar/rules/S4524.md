# S4524 — "default" clauses should be first or last

## Why
The rule fires when a `default` arm sits in the middle of a `switch`
statement or expression. Readers scan switches top-down expecting the order
to mirror priority; a buried `default` reads like just another case, so the
"everything else" behavior gets missed in review — and in switch
expressions, arm order also affects which pattern wins.

## Do this
1. Move the `default` arm to the last position (the conventional spot) or
   first, whichever the codebase uses — last is the common choice.
2. In switch expressions, double-check that moving it doesn't change which
   arm matches first; `default` / `_` matches everything, so it must stay
   where it can't shadow more specific arms — last is safe.
3. If the middle placement was grouping related cases for readability, add a
   brief comment at the new position instead.

## AVOID
- Do not duplicate the default arm at both ends "for safety" — two defaults
  is a compile error in some forms and confusion in all of them.
- Do not convert `default:` to `case _:` in the middle and claim the rule no
  longer applies; it's the same buried catch-all.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
