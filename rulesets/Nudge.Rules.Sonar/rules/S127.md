# S127 — "for" loop stop conditions should be invariant

## Why
`for (int i = 0; i < list.Count; i++)` re-evaluates the bound every iteration —
if the body mutates the collection, the bound shifts under the loop, causing
skipped elements, infinite loops, or `IndexOutOfRangeException`. Even when the
body is safe, it's needless per-iteration work and it forces every reader to
prove the body doesn't mutate the collection.

## Do this
1. Hoist the bound into a local before the loop:
   `int count = list.Count; for (int i = 0; i < count; i++)`.
2. If the body intentionally mutates the collection, say so explicitly —
   iterate over a snapshot (`ToList()`/`ToArray()`) or restructure to a `while`
   loop whose changing condition is the point.
3. In hot loops this also removes a per-iteration call; measure if it matters.

## AVOID
- Do not "hoist" by copying the bound expression into a lambda or local
   function that is still invoked per iteration — the condition is still
   evaluated every time; evaluate it once, before the loop.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
