# S2251 — A "for" loop update clause should move the counter in the right direction

## Why
In `for (int i = 0; i < n; i--)` the update moves the counter *away* from the
exit condition: the loop either never terminates or never executes its body
meaningfully. This isn't a subtle off-by-one — it's an infinite loop or dead
code, usually from a copy-paste where the direction wasn't flipped.

## Do this
1. Read the loop's condition and make the update move the counter toward it:
   `i++` for `i < n`, `i--` for `i > 0`.
2. If the loop was intentionally counting the "wrong" way, then the condition
   is the bug, not the update — fix the condition instead.
3. Add or extend a test that runs the loop to completion; a hanging test would
   have caught this immediately.

## AVOID
- Do not "fix" it by adding a `break` on an iteration cap inside the body —
   that papers over the wrong update and leaves the loop's contract a lie.
   Make the update and the condition agree.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
