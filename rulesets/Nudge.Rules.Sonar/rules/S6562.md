# S6562 — Always set the "DateTimeKind" when creating new "DateTime" instances

## Why
`new DateTime(2026, 10, 2)` creates a `DateTimeKind.Unspecified` value, and
`Unspecified` is the worst of all worlds: `ToUniversalTime()` *assumes*
it's local, `ToLocalTime()` *assumes* it's UTC, and serialization
round-trips guess. Kind bugs surface as off-by-timezone-hours errors that
only reproduce in certain deployments.

## Do this
1. Pass the kind explicitly:
   `new DateTime(y, m, d, h, mi, s, DateTimeKind.Utc)` — or
   `DateTime.SpecifyKind(existing, DateTimeKind.Utc)` when wrapping a value
   you received.
2. Decide the canonical kind for the codebase (UTC is the usual choice) and
   construct all stored/transmitted timestamps in it.
3. For new code, prefer `DateTimeOffset`, which carries the offset and makes
   the whole question moot.

## AVOID
- Do not call `DateTime.SpecifyKind(value, DateTimeKind.Local)` on a value
  that's actually UTC just to silence the rule — mislabeling the kind is
  worse than leaving it unspecified; the conversions will now be
  confidently wrong.
- Do not "fix" it by converting everything through `.ToUniversalTime()` on
  Unspecified values; that conversion *assumes local*, baking the same
  guess into the data.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
