# S1200 — Classes should not be coupled to too many other classes

## Why
A class coupled to too many other classes is hard to
understand, hard to test (every dependency needs a mock), and brittle:
a change in any of the dozen collaborators can ripple into it. High
fan-out usually means the class has several jobs wearing one name.

## Do this
1. List the class's dependencies and group them by the responsibility
   they serve — the groups are usually hidden sub-responsibilities.
2. Extract a facade or coordinator with a narrow interface for each
   group, or split the class along those responsibility lines.
3. Inject the narrower abstractions (interfaces) rather than concrete
   types, so tests and future changes touch one seam.

## AVOID
- Do not hide the dependencies behind a service locator
  (`IServiceProvider.GetService` everywhere) or `dynamic` — the coupling
  is still there, just invisible to the analyzer and to readers.
- Do not introduce a "god facade" that re-exports all twelve
  dependencies unchanged; the fan-out must actually shrink.

- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`. Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
