# S2178 — Short-circuit logic should be used in boolean contexts

## Why
& and | in a boolean context evaluate both sides unconditionally — unlike
&& and ||, there is no short-circuit, so the right side runs even when the
left already decided the outcome. That means wasted work and, worse,
NullReferenceExceptions and side effects that short-circuiting would have
skipped. This rule flags non-short-circuit operators on booleans.

## Do this
1. Replace & with && and | with || in boolean expressions.
2. Before flipping, check whether the right side has a side effect the code was (accidentally or deliberately) relying on — if it does, hoist that side effect into its own statement above the condition instead of depending on non-short-circuit evaluation.
3. Keep & / | only for actual bitwise work on integral types, where short-circuiting doesn't apply.

## AVOID
- Do not keep & and 'fix' it by adding a null check before the expression — the operator still evaluates both sides, and the next reader will still trip on it.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
