# S3626 — Jump statements should not be redundant

## Why
A `break` as the last statement of a `switch` case, a `return;` at the end of
a `void` method, or a `continue;` as the last statement of a loop body does
nothing — control flow already goes there. They're noise that makes readers
hunt for a non-existent reason the jump exists.

## Do this
1. Delete the redundant jump statement.
2. If it was guarding a fallthrough you actually want, restructure so the
   intent is explicit (e.g. `goto case` in C#, where switch fallthrough is
   otherwise illegal).

## AVOID
- This rule is mechanical — there is no meaningful dodge. Just make sure
  you're deleting the *redundant* one: a `break` inside an `if` nested in a
  case, or a `return` inside a conditional at the end of a method, is
  load-bearing — only the unconditional trailing jump is dead.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
