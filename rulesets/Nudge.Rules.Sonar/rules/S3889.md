# S3889 — "Thread.Resume" and "Thread.Suspend" should not be used

## Why
`Thread.Suspend` freezes a thread at an arbitrary instruction — possibly
mid-`lock`, mid-`finally`, or mid-allocation — and `Thread.Resume`
unfreezes it with no knowledge of what state was captured. The pair is
deprecated because it deadlocks unpredictably (suspend a thread holding a
lock your resuming thread needs) and corrupts invariants; both methods are
obsolete in modern .NET for good reason.

## Do this
1. Replace the suspend/resume handshake with a cooperative signal:
   `ManualResetEventSlim` (`Wait` / `Set` / `Reset`) or a `SemaphoreSlim`.
2. If the goal is cancellation rather than pausing, use a
   `CancellationToken` plumbed through the worker loop.
3. For producer/consumer coordination, prefer `Monitor.Wait` / `Pulse`,
   `BlockingCollection`, or Channels over manual thread parking.

## AVOID
- Do not replace `Suspend` / `Resume` with `Thread.Interrupt` —
  interrupting a thread at an arbitrary point has the same state-corruption
  problem.
- Do not spin-wait on a boolean flag (`while (!go) {}`) as the
  "cooperative" replacement; that's a CPU-burning busy loop, not
  coordination — use a wait handle.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
