# S3329 — Cipher Block Chaining IVs should be unpredictable

## Why
In CBC mode, a predictable or reused IV enables chosen-plaintext attacks that
recover plaintext (the BEAST class of attacks). The IV does not need to be
secret, but every encryption operation must use a fresh, random one — a static
IV defeats the purpose of the mode.

## Do this
1. Generate a new random IV for every encryption: let `Aes` create one via
   `GenerateIV()`, or fill 16 bytes from `RandomNumberGenerator`.
2. Transmit or store the IV alongside the ciphertext (prepend it; the
   decryptor reads it back) — never derive it from the key or a counter.
3. Verify the decrypt path reads the per-message IV rather than falling back
   to a shared constant.

## AVOID
- Do not use a fixed IV, a hardcoded byte array, or an IV derived
  deterministically from the key or password. A static IV is exactly the
  predictable IV the rule flags — secrecy of its value does not make it
  unpredictable.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
