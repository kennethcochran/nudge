# S5753 — ASP.NET Request Validation should not be disabled

## Why
Request validation is the framework's first-pass XSS filter, rejecting payloads
with unencoded markup before they reach your code. `[ValidateInput(false)]` or
`Request.Unvalidated` turns it off, leaving every downstream consumer to get
encoding right — a single missed `HtmlEncode` becomes stored XSS.

## Do this
1. Remove the opt-out and keep request validation enabled.
2. If one field legitimately needs markup (rich text), accept it and encode or
   sanitize it at the point of use with a real HTML sanitizer — don't disable
   validation for the whole request to accommodate one field.
3. Treat validation as defense in depth: HTML-encode on output regardless.

## AVOID
- Do not move the disable from the action into `web.config`
  (`validateRequest="false"`) — that's the same exposure widened to the whole
  application.
- Do not "compensate" with a hand-rolled regex stripping `<script>` —
  encodings, event handlers, and SVG vectors sail straight past it.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
