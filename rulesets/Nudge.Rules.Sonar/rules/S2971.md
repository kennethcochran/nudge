# S2971 — LINQ expressions should be simplified

## Why
The rule fires on LINQ chains that restate built-in operations:
`.Where(p).First()` (→ `.First(p)`), `.Where(p).Any()` (→ `.Any(p)`),
`.Count() > 0` (→ `.Any()`), `.Select(x => x)` (→ nothing). The longer form
does extra passes over the data — `.Where().First()` builds an intermediate
iterator — and buries the actual operation under ceremony.

## Do this
1. Apply the specific simplification the diagnostic suggests: fold the
   predicate into the terminal operator (`First`, `Single`, `Any`,
   `Count`), drop identity `Select`s, replace emptiness checks with `Any()`.
2. Watch for semantic edges: `.FirstOrDefault() != null` → `.Any()` is only
   equivalent when the element type can't itself be null in a meaningful
   way — check value types and nullable-reference intent before rewriting.
3. After simplifying, re-read the chain — one simplification often reveals
   another.

## AVOID
- Do not "simplify" by materializing early (`.ToList().First(p)`) — that
  defeats the laziness the simplification was preserving.
- Do not rewrite `.Count() > 0` as `.Count() != 0`; the point is that
  `Any()` short-circuits instead of counting everything.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
