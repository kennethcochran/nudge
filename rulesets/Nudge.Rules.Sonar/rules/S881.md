# S881 — Increment (++) and decrement (--) operators should not be used in a method call or mixed with other operators in an expression

## Why
`buffer[i++] = value` or `x = y++ * 2` forces the reader to simulate evaluation
order to know which value is used where — a classic source of off-by-one bugs,
and C#'s evaluation-order subtleties make the intent even harder to verify.

## Do this
1. Pull the increment or decrement out into its own statement on its own line,
   placed before or after the expression that uses the variable.
2. Re-read the surrounding code and confirm the value actually used is the one
   you intended, now that the ordering is explicit.

## AVOID
- Do not rewrite `i++` as `i += 1` (or `i = i + 1`) inside the same expression.
  The rule is about the side effect hiding inside a larger expression, not the
  `++` spelling — the compound-assignment form is the identical bug.
- Do not suppress with `#pragma warning disable` or `[SuppressMessage]`.
  Suppression of analyzer diagnostics is forbidden by the project's AGENTS.md.
